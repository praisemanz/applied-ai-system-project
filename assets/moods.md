# Mood and Activity Guide

## Focus and Study
For focus work, prefer low energy, low vocal density, and steady rhythm. Lo-fi hip hop, ambient, and downtempo electronic are reliable defaults. Avoid sudden dynamic changes, heavy distortion, or emotionally charged lyrics, which pull attention away from the task. Target tempo range is roughly 70 to 100 BPM.

## Workout and High Energy
For workouts, prioritize high tempo (130 to 180 BPM), high energy (above 0.7), and strong rhythmic drive. Pop punk, house, and drum and bass perform well. Lyrics with motivational or defiant themes can amplify perceived effort. Avoid mellow or atmospheric tracks unless the workout is a cooldown.

## Melancholic and Reflective
Reflective listening benefits from low valence (below 0.4), moderate to slow tempo, and rich acoustic or vocal texture. Indie folk, neo-soul ballads, and slower synthwave fit well. The goal is to support an emotion the listener already has, not to force a mood shift.

## Uplifting and Optimistic
Uplifting listening calls for high valence (above 0.65), moderate to high energy, and clear melodic hooks. Pop, neo-soul, and brighter house tracks work. Tempo can range from 95 to 130 BPM depending on whether the listener wants gentle uplift or active joy.

## Romantic and Intimate
Romantic playlists favor warm timbre, moderate tempo (80 to 110 BPM), and high valence with soft dynamics. Bossa nova, neo-soul, and acoustic indie folk are common picks. Avoid harsh distortion or aggressive percussion.

## Nostalgic and Cinematic
For nostalgia, lean on synthwave, dream pop, and 1980s-influenced styles. Production cues (gated reverb drums, analog synth pads, saxophone leads) carry the era. Tempo is moderate (95 to 115 BPM) and valence is bittersweet rather than purely happy or sad.
